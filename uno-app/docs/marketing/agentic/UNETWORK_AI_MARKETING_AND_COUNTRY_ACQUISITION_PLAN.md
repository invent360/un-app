# AI marketing agents and country acquisition plan

**Evaluation date:** 29 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Supplement the 2,500-licence campaign with cost-controlled AI marketing, advertising and onboarding.  
**Commercial model:** ULO 50%, UNO 40%, referral 10% of the defined distributable pool; UNO funds credits.  
**Status:** Recommendations and test budgets, not a purchased service, launched campaign or guaranteed forecast.

## 1. Recommendation

Use AI primarily to reduce content, qualification, follow-up and support work. Start acquisition through trusted community partners plus a small Facebook/Instagram test. Add one automation subscription only when it removes a measured bottleneck. Do not purchase several overlapping AI products or assume a monthly software fee includes advertising spend or a human campaign operator.

Recommended sequence:

1. Begin in Nigeria and the Philippines, the provisional first markets in the existing plan. Confirm actual task, device, account and redemption eligibility before recruiting.
2. Use one landing page system, country-specific pages, first-party event tracking and Meta Ads Manager. Keep partner recruitment as a separate measured channel.
3. Choose **HighLevel** when enquiry handling and onboarding are the bottleneck; choose **Plai** when ad setup and management are the bottleneck. Start with neither if native tools and a small manual pilot suffice.
4. Use **Sintra** only if regular content production is demonstrably consuming more time than the subscription saves. Defer Madgicx until spending and conversion volume justify it.
5. Evaluate success through first accepted work and D7/D30 retained productivity. A copied licence code, form submission or app download is not the campaign outcome.

The supplied LevelUp URL is an October 2026 Dallas conference website, not the software subscription. Conference attendance is not recommended as a cost-effective acquisition expense for this campaign. Evaluate HighLevel itself separately. [S1]

## 2. What “the agent handles marketing” actually means

| Service category | Work it can automate | Work still required |
|---|---|---|
| Content assistant | Drafts, translations, graphics, social calendars | Accurate offer, local review, distribution and measurement |
| CRM/conversation automation | Forms, segmentation, approved FAQs, reminders, handoff | Traffic acquisition, consent, reliable activation signals |
| AI ad-management software | Campaign preparation, creative variants, optimization within enabled controls | Ad account, payment method, media budget, objectives and supervision |
| Managed service using AI and people | Campaign execution and reporting under a contract | Business approvals, product readiness and accountability for outcomes |

No provider reviewed establishes a guarantee of 250 productive Unetwork operators per week. Vendor conversion claims and case studies from unrelated businesses are not transferable forecasts.

### HighLevel: where it helps and what it costs

A proposed starting configuration is one campaign sub-account with country/language tags, rather than one paid AI instance per country. Confirm that needed integrations and permissions are supported before selecting a tier. The public pricing page distinguishes API tiers; Starter should not be assumed to provide every endpoint needed by the Rust application. [S2]

Illustrative fixed subscriptions: Starter alone $97; with Growth $147; with Unlimited $194. Adding WhatsApp makes the latter two $157/$204. This excludes messages, calls, model usage outside the bundle, media and implementation. Growth includes 1,000 conversational responses; at 1,000 leads/month and six responses each, that allowance is inadequate. Agent Studio remains metered and telecom charges remain separate even on Unlimited. [S3–S4]

Use it to answer approved questions, collect eligibility information, route users to official installation/KYC, and follow up on verified milestones. Its Ad Manager can manage campaigns, with ad spend paid separately. It still requires account setup and an accountable operator. [S4]

### Plai: closer to automated advertising

Brand includes a workspace and ad-management tools; the page differentiates its optimization controls from higher tiers. The same page contains multiple offer blocks, so confirm the exact checkout plan. Its “$20k managed ad spend included” wording is a management allowance, not $20,000 of free advertising; its help documentation confirms that platforms charge media separately. The managed service starts at $3,000/month and requires a written scope. [S5–S6]

For this campaign, reject that managed-service price unless measured contribution can support it. At 1,000 retained acquisitions a month, $3,000 adds $3 acquisition cost each before media, credits or support. Cancelling Plai does not itself pause the ads according to its terms; retain direct control of platform budgets. [S14]

### Selection test

Ask each shortlisted vendor to demonstrate, using a non-production test account: country restrictions, approval before publication, hard budget caps, lead export, multilingual corrections, conversion-event ingestion, removal of access, and stopping all campaigns. Require an itemized quote for subscription, onboarding, media markup, usage, language support and human involvement. Keep ownership of the domain, ad accounts, audience data and creative assets.

### Meta campaign design

Start with two country-separated ad sets and two accurate creative concepts per market. Avoid fragmenting a small budget into many languages, devices and audiences simultaneously. Use explicit location/age controls supported by the account; verify delivered geography and control behaviour rather than assuming every audience suggestion is a strict boundary.

Test a website eligibility form first. Instant Forms or click-to-message can be alternatives, but compare their downstream activation and retention, not only cost per lead. Native automation may optimize toward cheap form completions unless deeper outcomes are measured. Feed permitted, consented conversion signals back through the supported integration where feasible; never send KYC documents, wallet credentials, lease codes or sensitive reward details to an advertising platform.

Prefer Leads/conversion objectives over buying traffic solely for clicks. Do not claim official app-install attribution unless the mobile application owner provides the necessary integration and permissions. A campaign website event is not a mobile activation event.

Use one verified explanation, one real demonstration and one transparent cost/reward FAQ. Human review must approve earnings language and translations. Do not use fabricated AI testimonials or AI avatars pretending to be actual paid participants.

### Policy and product fit

Facebook availability in a country is not approval for this particular offer. Direct retrieval of the detailed Meta cryptocurrency advertising policy was blocked during this review; no approval or exemption is asserted. Obtain classification of the actual offer and landing page, including any crypto, income-opportunity or employment implications. Do not rename or conceal the product to evade a restriction.

WhatsApp’s retrieved official policy requires permission to contact users, honors opt-outs, governs templates and the 24-hour response window, and requires an escalation route for automation. It also restricts currency-related promotion and certain business models. Confirm that this licence/reward offer is permitted before using it commercially there; fall back to the web support flow if it is not. [S12]

Verify Unetwork’s own authorization for promotion/subleasing and the precise rights granted to the ULO. The existing plan’s platform-permission gate remains applicable. No agent subscription resolves that requirement.

## 6. What the AI should do

| Step | Automated activity | Human/backend boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Draft localized explainers and approved ad variants | Human approves claims and first publication |
| Qualify | Ask adult status, country, eligible device, internet/data limits and cost understanding | Deterministic eligibility rules; no AI invention of supported countries |
| Explain | Answer from a dated, approved knowledge base | Escalate missing/conflicting answers; do not promise earnings |
| Onboard | Share official installation and verification links | KYC stays with official provider; no collection of documents by marketing bot |
| Issue | Request next authorized step from backend | Only repaired, authenticated backend reserves/issues; AI never accesses inventory secrets directly |
| Follow up | Reminders based on consent and verified milestones | Hard frequency limits and immediate opt-out |
| Retain | Ask about installation/task problems and route support | Human handles disputes, withdrawals and identity issues |
| Optimize | Summarize cost and retention by country/channel/cohort | Spend increases require bounded rules and accountable review |

Use stages: opted in → eligible → reserved → issued → upstream activated → first accepted work → D7 productive → D30 retained. Connect HighLevel or another CRM only after the corresponding backend events are reliable. The audited application presently has schema, authorization and claim-state defects; no AI agent should be given unrestricted control of it.

Publish an approved fact sheet covering genuine current tasks, country/device limits, the 50/40/10 agreement, credit funding duration/cap, other participant costs and variable rewards. A task described as projected stays excluded from earnings claims until validated.

Draft message, subject to authorization and local validation: “Use your existing eligible device to participate in supported network tasks. We provide access to a licence and cover the agreed credit costs. Rewards are small and variable; internet, power and time costs may remain. Check eligibility and the full terms before joining.”

## 7. Cost and acquisition economics

The correct metric is fully loaded cost per retained productive ULO:

`CAC_D30 = (media + allocated software + creative + onboarding labour + messaging + acquisition bounties) / distinct D30-retained productive ULOs`

Attribute expenses to the acquisition cohort even when retention is measured later. Recurring referral share belongs in operating contribution, not a second acquisition charge unless there is a separate bounty. Do not count the same participant twice across paid and partner sources.

Let P be verified monthly distributable pool per active licence, C credit cost paid by UNO and S recurring support/operations per active licence:

`Monthly UNO contribution M = 0.40 × P − C − S`

`Contribution LTV over H months = sum(survival_probability[m] × M[m])`

Subtract any costs incurred for inactive licences separately. Set affordable CAC below contribution LTV after overhead and reserve. When contribution is nonpositive, cheaper leads cannot repair ongoing losses.

Illustration only: C=$1.99 and S=$0.25 are unverified planning inputs, not established platform prices.

| Monthly pool P | Monthly UNO contribution | Six-month contribution with no churn, before acquisition/fixed overhead |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $3 | −$1.04 | −$6.24 |
| $6 | $0.16 | $0.96 |
| $9 | $1.36 | $8.16 |
| $15 | $3.76 | $22.56 |

This explains why a $3,000/month agency service is difficult to justify. Even at 1,000 D30 acquisitions per month, its $3 fee allocation alone exceeds the $0.96 optimistic six-month contribution in the $6-pool case. If credits actually cost $0 or $3.99, contribution changes dollar-for-dollar; confirm costs before choosing software.

At 1,000 retained acquisitions/month, $97 software adds $0.097 per acquisition; at 100 it adds $0.97; at 25 it adds $3.88. Tools are proportionately most expensive during a small pilot. A $194 bundle doubles those figures. Reuse one brand/account setup across markets where permitted rather than multiplying subscriptions.

### Can ads produce 250 per week?

Mathematically yes at some combination of conversion and spend; there is no evidence yet that this campaign can do so profitably.

| Hypothetical lead cost | Cost per D7 productive ULO at 30% lead→D7 | At 15% lead→D7 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $0.10 | $0.33 | $0.67 |
| $0.25 | $0.83 | $1.67 |
| $0.50 | $1.67 | $3.33 |
| $1.00 | $3.33 | $6.67 |

To produce 250 new D7 productive ULOs requires about 834 opted-in leads/week at 30% conversion or 1,667 at 15%. At $0.25/lead that implies approximately $209 or $417 weekly media spend, before all other costs. These are sensitivity calculations, not country CPL benchmarks. D30 retention will make retained CAC higher.

For 250 **net** weekly additions: `required gross productive additions = 250 + existing productive participants lost that week`. At 1,000 existing productive participants and 2% weekly attrition, 270 additions are needed. At 30% conversion that means 900 leads, not 834. Observe cohort lags; week-one leads cannot have week-one D30 outcomes.

## 8. A controlled pilot and scale decision

Do not spend the learning budget until the offer is authorized, at least a small controlled product pilot has validated participant economics, and the claim/security blockers are resolved or a secure authorized manual process is available.

Suggested two-country, 14-day learning envelope—not a promise of acquisitions:

| Item | Planning cap |
|---|---:|
| Media: $10/day/country × 2 × 14 | $280 |
| One optional software month | $97 |
| Local moderation/translation assistance | $120 |
| Messaging/usage allowance | $30 |
| Creative production allowance | $50 |
| Contingency | $23 |
| **Marketing learning cap** | **$600** |

Credit funding, recurring operations, tax and additional internal labour are not included in the $600 and must be budgeted separately. Record actual human hours even if the UNO does the work. Example: 50 funded licences at an assumed $1.99 plus $0.25 support require another $112 for one month; the price and billing unit still need confirmation. A learning budget may deliberately incur losses; it is not the ongoing sustainable acquisition budget.

Days 1–3: approve localized pages/copy, test opt-in and events, recruit initial moderator partners. Days 4–14: run the two ad sets and partner flows with separate attribution; review complaints and broken onboarding daily. Follow cohorts through D7 and D30 even after acquisition pauses.

Stop immediately on false claims, failed eligibility/withdrawal assumptions, unauthorized issuance or broken attribution. Set a loss budget per country before starting. As a management rule, pause and investigate a creative after it spends twice the affordable D7 CAC without a mature D7 result; account for the seven-day observation delay. This is a budget safeguard, not a statistically conclusive test. Small samples cannot identify stable national acquisition costs.

Expand only if fully loaded retained CAC fits conservative contribution LTV, participant net benefit is positive, support is sustainable and comparable incumbent task productivity does not deteriorate. Increase winning spend gradually under a weekly cap; reassess after each cohort. Add Kenya/Ghana or India/Bangladesh according to verified local support and results, not because the original quota table must be filled.

## 9. Purchase decision

**Now:** native Meta tools, community partners, accurate landing pages and dependable measurement. AI can draft content under review.

**First subscription if support is the bottleneck:** HighLevel Starter plus measured pay-per-use AI or the appropriate bundle; demonstrate the required backend integration first.

**First subscription if ad operation is the bottleneck:** a single-month Plai Brand trial, conditional on sufficient targeting/control features. Compare against native Meta management. Do not add both by default.

**Later:** Madgicx for a sufficiently large Meta operation; a creative tool only if creative throughput limits performance. A $3,000+ managed service is a poor starting fit for the currently unproven low-reward model.

No subscriptions, messages or ads were purchased or launched in this evaluation.
